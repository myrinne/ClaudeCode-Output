## Corrections (wrong answers only)

### 9. A 30-year-old male presents to the emergency department with rhinorrhea and excessive lacrimation for the last 30 minutes, after excessive inhalation of an ammonia-containing cleaner one hour ago. Which finding would best predict future development of chronic lung disease?
- **Your answer:** B - Reduced lung capacity on presentation to the emergency department
- **Key:** C - Injury to the bronchial lining on bronchoscopy
- **Why:** Best predictor of chronic lung disease after ammonia inhalation is the *depth of airway injury* seen on bronchoscopy (sloughed/ulcerated bronchial mucosa). Initial lung capacity, SpO2 and facial burns reflect severity at presentation but predict poorly.

### 11. The pathognomonic feature of lead-induced renal disease found on biopsy is:
- **Your answer:** A - Interstitial fibrosis with tubular atrophy
- **Key:** C - No pathognomonic feature
- **Why:** Lead nephropathy has no pathognomonic histology. Intranuclear inclusion bodies occur in the *proximal tubule* (not glomeruli) and are seen in acute lead exposure; chronic disease = interstitial fibrosis with tubular atrophy, which is non-specific.

### 12. Which of the following statements regarding hydrofluoric acid (HF) is true?
- **Your answer:** A - HF does not cause immediate pain
- **Key:** C - Calcium gluconate gel (2.5%) can be used as the first aid kit
- **Why:** HF: 2.5% calcium gluconate gel is first aid. Option A is false: pain can be delayed with dilute HF, but concentrated HF hurts immediately; skin penetration is high (F- binds Ca2+/Mg2+), and HF is NOT harmless below 50%.

### 13. Which of the following statements is FALSE regarding carbon tetrachloride (CCl4)?
- **Your answer:** E - Respiratory effects can occur through various means of exposure such as inhalation, oral, parenteral
- **Key:** B - Acute hepatic injury usually manifests before CNS effects have subsided
- **Why:** Question asks FALSE. In CCl4 poisoning CNS depression comes first (hours); hepatic injury appears *delayed* (24-72 h) after CNS effects have subsided, so B is the false statement. Read the negation first.

### 15. The amount of a substance that entered the body and was taken up by organs or particular tissues is called:
- **Your answer:** D - Effective dose
- **Key:** C - Internal dose
- **Why:** Internal dose = amount absorbed into the body. Effective (biologically effective) dose = amount reaching the target site. Administered/applied dose = amount given or contacted.

### 16. Effect of chronic exposure to inorganic mercury:
- **Your answer:** A - Gingivostomatitis
- **Key:** B - Acute tubular necrosis
- **Why:** Key = B (ATN) but the item is poorly built (other options not recorded). Chronic inorganic Hg classically gives gingivostomatitis, tremor, erethism and proteinuria/nephrotic syndrome, so your answer is clinically defensible. Just know the key; do not build reasoning on it.

### 17. Which of the following statements is TRUE regarding the toxic effects of metals?
- **Your answer:** D - Anemia can develop with Pb poisoning through inhibition of delta-aminolevulinic acid dehydratase (ALAD) and hyperactivation of ferrochelatase in heme biosynthesis
- **Key:** C - The toxic mechanism is usually via reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation, enzyme inactivation, and suppression of antioxidant defense
- **Why:** Lead inhibits ALAD *and ferrochelatase* (it does not hyperactivate it), so D is false. C (ROS generation, enzyme inactivation, antioxidant depletion) is the general mechanism of metal toxicity.

### 19. According to the WHO classification, substances with an oral LD50 for the rat of 100 mg/kg body weight are classified as:
- **Your answer:** C - Slightly hazardous
- **Key:** A - Moderately hazardous
- **Why:** A (moderately) is correct per the source key (Remed Agt 2022) and the WHO 2009 *solid* range (class II = 50-500 mg/kg). 'Slightly hazardous' = class III (>500 mg/kg solids).
- **Note (physical state):** the question does not say solid or liquid. WHO 2009 oral rat ranges (from memory, not re-checked against the WHO document): Ib 5-50 solid / 20-200 liquid; II 50-500 / 200-2000; III >500 / >2000. A liquid at 100 mg/kg would be Ib (highly hazardous). Use the key (A) for the exam.

### 21. In metal fume fever, which one of the following statements is the best fit?
- **Your answer:** C - Onset is typically immediate
- **Key:** A - Risk is increased with increasing thickness of metal in cutting operations
- **Why:** Metal fume fever: onset 4-12 h after exposure (not immediate); tolerance *does* develop during the week and is lost over the weekend (Monday fever); self-limiting (not progressive); typically zinc oxide (cadmium gives pneumonitis). By elimination A is best.

### 24. Which of the following gases is a chemical asphyxiant?
- **Your answer:** A - Nitrogen
- **Key:** B - Hydrogen sulfide
- **Why:** Chemical asphyxiants (H2S, CO, HCN) block O2 transport/utilisation. N2, CO2 and methane are *simple* asphyxiants (displace O2). Ammonia is an irritant.

### 27. When the insecticides chlorpyrifos and diazinon are absorbed into an organism, what would be the most likely combined effect?
- **Your answer:** E - Potentiation
- **Key:** A - Additive
- **Why:** Chlorpyrifos and diazinon are both organophosphates acting on the same target (AChE), so effects are *additive*. Potentiation = a non-toxic agent amplifies another's toxicity (e.g. isopropanol + CCl4).

### 28. In dose–response relationships, the term "threshold" refers to which of the following?
- **Your answer:** A - The degree to which a substance exhibits a dose–response relationship
- **Key:** E - The dose below which the probability of an individual responding is zero  *(new verified key)*
- **Why:** Threshold = dose below which the probability of response is zero (Casarett & Doull). NOAEL is the experimental estimate of the threshold; 'degree of dose-response' is not a definition of anything.

### 30. Which organs are least involved in systemic toxicity?
- **Your answer:** A - Lungs and reproductive systems
- **Key:** D - Muscle and bone
- **Why:** Target organs of systemic toxicity: CNS, liver, kidney, blood, lung, reproductive system. Muscle and bone are the least involved.

### 32. A 47-year-old firefighter with 15 years of service has elevated serum cholesterol and mildly decreased kidney function. He participates in routine foam-based firefighting exercises. Laboratory analysis reveals elevated perfluorooctanoic acid (PFOA). Which health concern is most associated with chronic occupational PFAS exposure?
- **Your answer:** A - Hypersensitivity pneumonitis
- **Key:** E - Kidney and testicular cancer
- **Why:** PFOA: IARC Group 1 (kidney and testicular cancer). Raised cholesterol and reduced renal function in the vignette fit. Hypersensitivity pneumonitis, asthma and bone marrow aplasia are not PFAS endpoints.

### 33. A 52-year-old welder in a stainless-steel plant reports headache, giddiness, and hallucinations. Over time his family notes bizarre behavior and gait instability. Examination shows intention tremor. What is the most likely metal causing this neuropsychiatric disorder?
- **Your answer:** D - Cadmium
- **Key:** A - Mercury vapor
- **Why:** Key = mercury vapor (erethism: hallucinations, bizarre behaviour, intention tremor). Note: this picture in a stainless-steel welder is also classic *manganism*, but manganese is not an option. Cadmium causes kidney/bone/lung effects, not this neuropsychiatric syndrome.

### 34. A 35-year-old paint factory worker presents with weakness, paresthesia, and decreased distal vibration sensation. He mixes solvent-based coatings containing n-hexane. Which peripheral nerve pathology is most characteristic of chronic exposure to this solvent?
- **Your answer:** B - Patchy immune-mediated demyelination
- **Key:** A - Axonal swelling with giant axonal degeneration
- **Why:** n-Hexane -> 2,5-hexanedione -> *giant axonal swelling* with distal axonopathy. Immune demyelination is GBS/CIDP; Schwann-cell segmental demyelination is a different pattern (e.g. diphtheria).

### 39. An organophosphate sprayer has SLUDGE symptoms. What is the pathophysiology?
- **Your answer:** C - Muscarinic antagonism in peripheral nerves
- **Key:** B - Muscarinic activation in peripheral nerves
- **Why:** SLUDGE = muscarinic *activation* (excess ACh from AChE inhibition). Muscarinic antagonism is what atropine does (the antidote).

### 41. A worker has hyperkeratosis of the hands and feet and hand tremor. What is the likely occupational exposure?
- **Your answer:** D - Manganese
- **Key:** A - Arsenic
- **Why:** Hyperkeratosis of palms/soles + tremor/neuropathy points to chronic arsenic. Manganese gives parkinsonism/psychosis, no hyperkeratosis.

### 42. Samples were collected for a specific operation over three consecutive days, giving a TWA for lead oxide of 0.09 mg/m³ (TLV-TWA = 0.05 mg/m³; PEL = 0.2 mg/m³). You should:
- **Your answer:** C - Recommend rotation of workers
- **Key:** A - Recommend respirators until engineering controls can be implemented
- **Why:** TWA 0.09 is above TLV-TWA (0.05) but below the legal PEL (0.2). Legal does not mean safe. Hierarchy: engineering controls first, respirators as interim. Rotation just spreads lead exposure and is not an acceptable control for lead.

### 44. Which of the following statements is FALSE regarding biomarkers?
- **Your answer:** E - Objective, quantifiable characteristics of biological processes
- **Key:** A - It cannot be used to estimate exposure
- **Why:** Question asks FALSE. Biomarkers *can* be used to estimate exposure (biomonitoring/BEI), so A is the false one. E is just the definition of a biomarker.

### 49. According to ACGIH, ____ represents the levels of determinants that are most likely to be observed in specimens collected from healthy workers who have been exposed to chemicals to the same extent as workers with inhalation exposure at the TLV.
- **Your answer:** C - Biomarker of exposure
- **Key:** A - Biological Exposure Index
- **Why:** The *BEI* (ACGIH) is the determinant level expected in healthy workers exposed at the TLV. A generic 'biomarker of exposure' has no such reference value.

### 50. The measure of all the exposures of an individual in a lifetime and how those exposures relate to health is the definition of:
- **Your answer:** A - Cumulative exposure
- **Key:** B - Exposome
- **Why:** Exposome = totality of exposures over a lifetime and their relation to health. Cumulative exposure is the dose integral for one agent (e.g. fibre-years).

### 52. Which of the following statements is FALSE regarding biomarkers?
- **Your answer:** D - Biomarkers of effect should reflect early reversible changes in the organism
- **Key:** B - Typically, the agent or parent compound is always detected in a biological specimen
- **Why:** Question asks FALSE. B says the parent compound is *always* detected: false, it is often a metabolite (e.g. hippuric acid for toluene, mandelic acid for styrene).

### 53. Which of the following is TRUE of general ventilation systems?
- **Your answer:** C - They should be used when a contaminant cannot be controlled by substitution, changing the process, isolation, or enclosure
- **Key:** E - They should be used only when small quantities of air contaminants are being released into the work environment
- **Why:** General (dilution) ventilation fits only small quantities of low-toxicity contaminants. D describes local exhaust ventilation (capture at source) and C misstates the control hierarchy. Highly toxic dust/fume needs LEV, not dilution.

### 56. A group of workers works simultaneously with both toluene and xylene. A thorough occupational hygiene assessment showed both toluene and xylene levels are 60% of the occupational exposure limit (OEL) 8-hour TWA. Which statement is the best fit?
